Seventy-two learners at Thuthuka Primary School in Tembisa went home warm on Thursday.

On Thursday 30 July 2026, the Fudumala Ubuntu Challenge arrived at Thuthuka Primary School in the Emfihlweni section of Tembisa and handed over 72 school jerseys. Every jersey came sealed, in the school's own maroon and gold, with a small white label on the front carrying one learner's name, their school and their grade. Not a box of stock. Seventy-two named children.

It was the second handover of the week. On Tuesday 28 July, 100 jerseys went to learners at Sindawonye Primary School in Mamelodi, backed by Investec together with private sponsors. Two schools, three days, 172 jerseys.

The queue along the hall wall at Thuthuka Primary School, 30 July 2026. (Learner faces blurred to protect privacy.)

Every jersey is labelled before it leaves. Name, school, grade. Thuthuka Primary School, 30 July 2026.

Winter on the Highveld does not end when July does

There is a habit, every year, of treating the end of July as the end of winter. The jersey drives wind down, the cold-weather appeals go quiet, and the assumption sets in that the worst is behind us. The numbers do not agree.

Conditions and forecast for Tembisa, Gauteng, recorded on 30 July 2026 (Open-Meteo and World Weather Online).

Tembisa fell to about 10°C overnight before the handover and climbed to roughly 20°C in the afternoon under a clear sky, with the humidity dropping as low as 18 percent. The night after the handover was forecast colder still, near 6°C, and the first two nights of August colder again, close to 5°C. Across the following fortnight the forecast carries no rain at all.

That is the shape of August on the Highveld. Cold mornings, dry air, and wind. Berg winds, the warm dry winds that blow down off the escarpment through the South African winter, pull what little moisture is left out of the air and push fire danger up across the interior. Children walk to school in it. The 6am walk in August is not gentler than the 6am walk in June, it is simply less talked about.

What some learners were wearing against the cold before Thursday. Thuthuka Primary School, 30 July 2026.

A school jersey is a uniform, not a substitute

Learners arrived at the handover in what they had. A navy fleece worn thin and pilled. An old grey puffer jacket carried over one arm. A faded pink top doing the work a jersey should be doing. None of those children were cold because nobody cared. They were cold because a school jersey costs money a household does not always have in the middle of a school year.

This is where warmth and dignity stop being two separate things. A jacket keeps a child warm. A school jersey, in the school's own colours, with the school crest and the words Aim High stitched on the chest, does something a jacket cannot. It puts the child in line with everyone else. There is no longer a visible difference between the learner whose parents could buy the uniform and the learner whose parents could not. Nobody has to explain anything at assembly. Nobody gets asked why they are dressed differently.

That is the whole point of the name on the label. It is not a donation handed to a crowd. It is one jersey, in one child's size, with one child's name on it. Fudumala comes from the Zulu word meaning to warm up, and the campaign has always meant both kinds of warmth: the warmth of the garment, and the warmth of being counted.

The Thuthuka Primary School crest and its motto, Aim High, on a jersey handed over on 30 July 2026.

Thank you to the sponsors who funded these 72 jerseys

The Thuthuka Primary School handover was funded by private sponsors of the Fudumala Ubuntu Challenge. People who saw a counter on a website, decided that one child was worth the price of a jersey, and paid for it. Most of them will never meet the learner whose name went onto the label they made possible.

Threads for iKasi Foundation extends its sincere gratitude to every sponsor who funded a jersey for Thuthuka Primary School, and to the school's staff, who opened their hall and organised their learners so that the handover could be done properly rather than quietly.

The Threads for iKasi Foundation team alongside the learners waiting their turn, Thuthuka Primary School, 30 July 2026. (Learner faces blurred to protect privacy.)

The Threads for iKasi Foundation team at Thuthuka Primary School, 30 July 2026.

201 of 1 000, and the cold has not finished

As at 30 July 2026, the Fudumala counter stood at 201 sponsorships towards the 2026 goal of 1 000. That is 799 learners still on the list, with August ahead of them.

The campaign does not close at the end of winter, because for the children still waiting, winter is exactly the point. If you have been meaning to sponsor a jersey, this is the part of the year when it matters most and when the fewest people are paying attention.

More from the day

Questions people ask about the Thuthuka handover

How many school jerseys were handed over at Thuthuka Primary School?

72 school jerseys were handed over to learners at Thuthuka Primary School in Tembisa on Thursday 30 July 2026. Each jersey was individually labelled with the learner's name, school and grade.

Where is Thuthuka Primary School?

Thuthuka Primary School is in the Emfihlweni section of Tembisa, Gauteng, at 547 Mngoma Street.

How cold was it in Tembisa on the day of the handover?

Tembisa dropped to a low of about 10°C overnight on 30 July 2026 and reached a daytime high of about 20°C, with clear skies, relative humidity as low as 18 percent and no rainfall. The following night was forecast to fall to about 6°C.

Why does Fudumala keep handing over jerseys in late July and August?

Winter on the Highveld does not end when July does. Overnight temperatures in Tembisa stay in single digits well into August, and August brings dry, windy conditions with almost no rainfall. A learner without a jersey in August is as cold as a learner without a jersey in June.

What is the August dry wind on the Highveld?

August on the South African Highveld is dry and windy. Berg winds, which are warm dry winds that blow down off the escarpment during the South African winter, strip moisture out of the air and raise fire danger across the interior. For a child walking to school, it means cold, dry wind with no rain to break it.

Who sponsored the Thuthuka Primary School handover?

The 72 school jerseys handed over at Thuthuka Primary School were funded by private sponsors of the Fudumala Ubuntu Challenge.

What did each learner receive?

Each learner received one school jersey in the Thuthuka Primary School colours, sealed and labelled with their own name, school and grade. No other items were included.

How many jerseys has the Fudumala Ubuntu Challenge reached in 2026?

As at 30 July 2026, the public Fudumala counter stood at 201 sponsorships towards the 2026 goal of 1 000, made up of 171 jersey sponsorships and 30 Fudumala Pack sponsorships.

How do I sponsor a school jersey through Fudumala?

Sponsorships are made online at fudumala.threadsforikasi.org/sponsor. The campaign accepts both individual and corporate sponsorships and is open on an ongoing basis.

Is Threads for iKasi Foundation a registered NPO?

Yes. Threads for iKasi Foundation is a registered South African non-profit organisation, NPO registration number 2024/352240/08.
